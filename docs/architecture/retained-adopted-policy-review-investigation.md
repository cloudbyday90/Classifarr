cap/eligibility, deterministic mode refusal, and genuinely rejected transient or
permanent AI operations. Each retains the deterministic candidate. Invocation
counts and provider/error observations depend on whether the service was entered.

The successful policy reevaluation already captures a private live numeric witness
and frozen review seed before adoption callbacks. Its stage association binds the
actual successful gate, exact policy candidate, destination/method/confidence,
typed item and loaded pool. The current observer additionally requires a later
invocation context and only retains a fulfilled AI result. Early returns never
prepare that observer; genuine failures never retain its fulfilled-result frame.

Consequently the final publisher finds no frame receipt on the retained candidate.

or incomplete evidence instead suppresses that fallback.

Fresh-input refusals are already different: they deliberately register forced
review gaps. Any extension must preserve their precedence, including when a
display-prefix change invalidates invocation proof but label-free numeric policy
evidence remains valid. Provider capability refusal fulfills normally and already
receives current review evidence.

## Contract limits and further choices

The smallest existing frame contract covers `prompt_confirm`. Policy-only
`prompt_select` and `auto_classify` have related reader mismatches but require a
separate action/source/reader expansion. Not every automatic result enters review;
that depends on ordinary safety gates. Failed/skipped reevaluation creates no new
adopted candidate and cannot promote a retained earlier result into fresh proof.

A separate policy-current check can reuse the successful evaluation independently
of AI entry, optional advice/profile or verification-label admission. Final
publication still requires exact apply selection after rollout fulfills. The
existing transactional INSERT, row-ID binding, permitted-question-destination

scope of correcting that inherited presentation was a separate decision, now

## Official research and recommendation

Sources were discovered and retrieved through GitHub/web MCP on November 13.
W3C PROV-DM distinguishes actual utilization, generation and derivation. That
supports distinguishing a successful policy evaluation from an unperformed or
failed AI operation; it does not prescribe Classifarr's status enumeration.
[W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/).

OWASP recommends matching decisions to the relevant request/context and enforcing
their complete semantics. Its fail-closed guidance concerns authorization. It
does not require discarding valid deterministic review evidence whenever optional
AI work is unavailable. These are application-specific design inferences, not a
new permission to route or learn.
[Output handling](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Decisions_And_Output_Handling_Cheat_Sheet.html),
[authorization](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html).

| Option | Benefit | Cost or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve absent-frame fallback | No behavior change | Can recommend original A after B was adopted |
| Always record a gap on skip/failure | Prevents original fallback | Discards valid current policy mechanics |
| Record genuine current policy or an explicit gap | Preserves useful policy evidence independently of AI availability | Requires exact candidate ownership and changes review fingerprints |

Recommended stack: successful live evaluation, exact private candidate binding,
closed current frame or gap, apply-only publication, transactional row binding,
existing shared review readers and completed-answer recovery before new validation.
Keep provider outcome/accounting and routing/learning authorization independent.
